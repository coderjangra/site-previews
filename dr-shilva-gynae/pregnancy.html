Pregnancy Care

Pregnancy care: what to expect

A general guide to antenatal visits, from your first appointment to the weeks after birth. Your own schedule may differ; it is set with you at your visits.

The antenatal journey

Five stages, one steady plan.

Each visit builds on the last. Bring your questions every time; nothing is too small to ask.

General information only, not medical advice. Tests and scans are advised by your doctor based on your health.

  1. As soon as you know

    First visit

    Confirming the pregnancy, going through your health history and any earlier pregnancies, and estimating your due date.

    • The date your last period started
    • Earlier reports and prescriptions
    • Questions about supplements and diet

  2. Weeks 1–13

    First trimester

    Early check-ups, with blood tests and an early scan as advised. Nausea and tiredness are common; mention anything that worries you.

  3. Weeks 14–27

    Second trimester

    Regular checks of blood pressure, weight and your baby's growth. A detailed scan of the baby is usually offered in this stage, as advised.

  4. Weeks 28–40

    Third trimester

    Visits become more frequent. You will talk about your baby's movements, signs of labour, your birth plan and the hospital.

  5. After birth

    Postnatal check

    A check on your recovery, feeding, contraception and how you are feeling emotionally.

What to bring

Your visit checklist

Keeping everything in one folder makes each visit quicker and helps the doctor see the full picture.

Papers

  • Earlier reports, scans and prescriptions
  • Your pregnancy file or card, if you have one
  • Blood group and any test results you already have

Details

  • The date your last period started
  • A list of medicines and supplements you take
  • Any allergies or past operations

Questions

  • Write them down as they come up
  • Ask about diet, travel, work and exercise
  • Ask what each test is for

Company

  • Your partner or a family member is welcome to come along [confirm]
  • Arrive a few minutes early for your appointment

Warning signs

When to call straight away

Call the clinic, or go to the nearest hospital emergency department, if you notice any of these during pregnancy.

  • Bleeding from the vagina
  • Severe or constant pain in the tummy
  • A gush or a steady leak of fluid
  • Your baby moving less than usual, or not at all, later in pregnancy
  • Severe headache, blurred vision or flashing lights
  • Sudden swelling of the face, hands or feet
  • Fever, or burning when you pass urine
  • Vomiting so much you cannot keep fluids down
  • Fainting, fits or difficulty breathing
  • Anything that feels wrong to you

In an emergency, don't wait for a WhatsApp reply. Go to the nearest hospital or call 112.

Questions

Pregnancy FAQs

When should I book my first pregnancy visit?

As soon as you have a positive pregnancy test, or earlier if you are planning a pregnancy and want advice before you conceive.

How often will I have check-ups?

It depends on your health and your baby's. Visits are usually spaced out early in pregnancy and become more frequent towards the end. Your schedule is set with you at your visits.

Where will my delivery take place?
