Treatments, explained

What each treatment at Kalia Dental involves, how many visits it usually takes and how to look after your teeth afterwards, in plain language.

General information only. The number of visits and the aftercare you need depend on your teeth and health, and are confirmed by Dr. Kalia after an examination. Always follow the instructions the clinic gives you.

Heavy bleeding that will not stop, or swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, needs urgent medical care. Go to the nearest hospital emergency department.

Prevention

Digital check-up & examination

What it is

A careful look at your teeth, gums, bite and the soft tissues of your mouth, to spot decay, gum problems or wear early. It is the right place to start if you have not seen a dentist in a while or are not sure what you need.

Usual visits

Usually 1 visit, often combined with a cleaning

Aftercare tips

  • Follow the home-care advice you are given at the visit.
  • Book your next check-up when advised. Many people are seen about every six months, but your interval may differ.
  • Do not wait for your next check-up if you notice new pain, sensitivity or bleeding gums.
Diagnosis

Digital X-rays

What it is

Images that show what cannot be seen by looking in the mouth: between the teeth, under old fillings, and the roots and bone. Digital images appear on screen, so they can be explained to you at the same appointment.

Usual visits

Taken during your appointment and usually done in a few minutes

Good to know

  • No special aftercare is needed.
  • Tell the clinic before an X-ray if you are, or might be, pregnant.
  • Bring any earlier X-rays or reports, as they help with comparison.
Prevention

Teeth cleaning: scaling & polishing

What it is

Removal of hardened deposits (tartar, also called calculus) and surface stains that brushing cannot shift. The clinic uses ultrasonic scaling followed by polishing. Regular cleaning helps keep gums healthy.

Usual visits

Usually 1 visit; heavy build-up may be split over 2

Aftercare tips

  • Some sensitivity or tender gums for a day or two is common.
  • Brush twice a day with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste, and clean between your teeth daily.
  • Keep brushing gently even if your gums bleed a little, and tell the clinic if the bleeding continues.
Prevention

Fluoride treatment

What it is

A fluoride gel or varnish painted onto the teeth to help strengthen enamel and lower the risk of decay. It is often used for children, and for adults who are more prone to cavities.

Usual visits

1 short visit, often after a cleaning; may be repeated as advised

Aftercare tips

  • Avoid eating, drinking or rinsing for as long as the clinic advises, often at least 30 minutes.
  • After a varnish, soft foods and no brushing until the time you are given are usually advised.
  • Keep using a fluoride toothpaste at home.
Repair

Tooth-coloured fillings

What it is

Composite resin, matched to the shade of your tooth, used to fill a cavity once the decay has been removed. The material is set hard with a special light.

Usual visits

Usually 1 visit per tooth or group of teeth

Aftercare tips

  • If the area was numbed, avoid chewing and hot drinks until the numbness wears off, so you do not bite your cheek or lip.
  • Some sensitivity to hot and cold for a few days can happen.
  • If your bite feels high or uneven once the numbness has gone, call the clinic so it can be adjusted.
Repair

Root canal treatment

What it is

Treatment for a tooth whose inner pulp is infected or inflamed. The infected pulp is cleared out, and the canals are cleaned and sealed. A crown is usually advised afterwards to protect the tooth.

Usual visits

Often 1 to 3 visits, plus a later visit for a crown if needed

Aftercare tips

  • Some tenderness when biting for a few days is common. Take only the medicines prescribed for you.
  • Avoid chewing hard food on that tooth until the final filling or crown is in place.
  • Keep all follow-up appointments, and contact the clinic if you notice swelling or the pain gets worse.
Repair & replace

Crowns & bridges

What it is

A crown is a cap that covers a damaged or weakened tooth. A bridge fills a gap by anchoring an artificial tooth to the neighbouring teeth.

Usual visits

Usually 2 visits: one to prepare the tooth and take measurements, one to fit

Aftercare tips

  • If you have a temporary crown, avoid sticky and very hard food on that side.
  • Clean under a bridge every day with a floss threader or interdental brush.
  • Avoid biting on very hard things such as ice or pen caps.
Replace

Dental implants

What it is

A small titanium or zirconia post placed in the jawbone to stand in for a missing tooth root. Once it has bonded with the bone, it can support a crown, bridge or denture. Whether an implant suits you depends on your bone and general health, which is checked first.

Usual visits

Several visits over a few months: assessment, placement, healing, then the new tooth

Aftercare tips

  • Eat soft food and avoid chewing on the area for the first days, as advised.
  • Avoid smoking, which can slow healing.
  • Clean around the implant as carefully as your natural teeth, and keep your review appointments.
Oral surgery

Tooth extraction

What it is

Removing a tooth that cannot be saved, for example because of severe decay or a crack. The area is numbed with a local anaesthetic before the tooth is removed.

Usual visits

Usually 1 visit, with a review if stitches were placed

Aftercare tips

  • Bite gently on the gauze for as long as advised, often 30 to 45 minutes.
  • For the first 24 hours, avoid spitting, rinsing hard, drinking through a straw and smoking, so the clot can settle.
  • Eat soft, cool food. From the next day, gentle warm salt-water rinses are often advised.
  • Contact the clinic if bleeding does not settle, or if pain or swelling gets worse after two or three days.
Children

Children's dentistry

What it is

Check-ups and care planned around children: explaining each step simply, looking for early decay and helping parents with brushing and diet. The clinic describes its children's care as child-friendly and preventive.

Usual visits

Short, regular visits. A first check-up is commonly suggested when the first tooth appears or by the first birthday

Tips for parents

  • Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste: a thin smear for children under 3, a pea-sized amount from 3 to 6.
  • Keep sugary snacks and drinks to mealtimes.
  • Avoid putting a child to bed with a bottle of milk or juice.
Appearance

Cosmetic dentistry

What it is

Treatments that change how teeth look, such as their shade or shape. Which options are suitable depends on the health of your teeth and gums, and is discussed after an examination. Ask the clinic which cosmetic treatments it offers.

Usual visits

Depends on the treatment; discussed at a consultation

Aftercare tips

  • Ask what your specific treatment needs, as aftercare differs between treatments.
  • Tea, coffee and tobacco can stain teeth and some dental materials over time.
  • Keep up regular check-ups and cleaning.
